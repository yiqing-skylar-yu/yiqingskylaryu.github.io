Teaching

Teaching portfolio for Yiqing Skylar Yu.

Teaching Philosophy

I teach students to think scientifically and become increasingly independent by making complex work manageable, active, applied, and connected to goals beyond a single course. Across courses and research mentoring, my goal is to help students become careful consumers and producers of evidence.

I use scaffolding not to lower expectations, but to make high expectations attainable. Students practice component skills, receive feedback, and then apply that feedback in more independent work.

How I Teach

01

Build Independence Through Scaffolding

In PSY 250, the semester-long research proposal is broken into manageable stages: assignment orientation, article review, literature search, research idea, hypothesis and variables, outlines, peer review, full introduction, final proposal, and presentation.

02

Think Scientifically

Students compare popular or public-facing science claims with original research articles, asking whether causal language is appropriate, whether limitations were omitted, and whether conclusions go beyond the design.

03

Learn Actively

I use think-pair-share, small-group discussion, applied examples, reflection questions, discussion boards, group problem solving, student-generated examples, and research-design activities.

04

Structure Collaboration

Group work is designed around clear expectations, defined responsibilities, peer evaluation, and safeguards against free riding. In online summer courses, I continue to refine how collaboration can remain useful without adding unnecessary coordination burden.

05

Connect Learning To Transferable Skills

Students practice critical thinking, project management, teamwork, communication, public speaking, evidence evaluation, and scientific literacy. A student does not need to become a psychologist for these skills to matter.

06

Practice Reflective Improvement

I use student feedback to revise pacing, scaffolding, readings, online structure, assignment instructions, and accessibility of learning activities while preserving core learning goals.

Students consistently identify knowledge, organization, critical thinking, engagement, feedback, and accessibility as strengths. PSY 250 evaluations directly support the value of the scaffolded research proposal, and PSY 317 evaluations show strong engagement with research design, open science, and critical thinking despite the substantial requirements of the lab format.

Feedback-Driven Revision

Teaching improvement is most meaningful when it preserves learning goals while reducing unnecessary barriers. In PSY 317, I have worked within a required lab structure to reduce unnecessary reading, improve pacing and scaffolding, and clarify why each component matters. In online courses, I continue to refine how group work, embedded reflection activities, and exam preparation can better support student learning.
