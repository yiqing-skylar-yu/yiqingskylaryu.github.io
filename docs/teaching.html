Teaching

Teaching Philosophy

I teach students to think scientifically, evaluate evidence critically, and become increasingly independent learners. My courses combine structured practice, active learning, and scaffolded projects so that students can build difficult skills before using them independently.

Whether or not students continue in psychology, I want them to leave my courses better able to ask good questions, evaluate claims, communicate evidence, and solve problems.

How I Teach

Build Independence Through Scaffolding

The PSY 250 research proposal is my clearest example. Students begin by evaluating a springboard article and searching the literature, then develop a research question, hypotheses, and variables. Through outlines, peer review, and instructor feedback, they build individual components before integrating them into a complete research proposal and presentation.

Teach Students to Trace Claims Back to Evidence

In one activity, students locate popular articles that make claims about detox diets, identify the scientific sources those articles cite, find the original research, and compare what the evidence supports with how the claim is presented publicly. The exercise turns scientific literacy into a concrete habit: follow a claim back to its evidence before deciding what to believe.

Learn Actively

I use think-pair-share, small-group discussion, student-generated examples, and applied research-design activities to give students repeated opportunities to practice reasoning rather than only listen to explanations. These activities ask students to explain concepts, evaluate evidence, make methodological decisions, and learn from how their classmates approach the same problem.

Connect Learning to Transferable Skills

Students practice critical thinking, project management, communication, teamwork, public speaking, and evidence evaluation throughout my courses. These skills remain useful whether students continue in psychology, pursue another field, or apply what they have learned outside the classroom.
